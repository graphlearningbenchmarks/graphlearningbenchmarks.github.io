abstract: Despite quick progress in the last few years, recent studies have shown
  that modern graph neural networks can still fail at very simple tasks, like detecting
  small cycles. This hints at the fact that current networks fail to catch information
  about the local structure, which is problematic if the downstream task heavily relies
  on graph substructure analysis, as in the context of chemistry. We propose a very
  simple correction to the now standard GIN convolution that enables the network to
  detect small cycles with nearly no cost in terms of computation time and number
  of parameters. Tested on real life molecule property datasets, our model consistently
  improves performance on large multi-tasked datasets over all baselines, both globally
  and on a per-task setting.
