abstract: 'Recently, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have significantly advanced the
  performance of machine learning tasks on graphs. However, this technological breakthrough
  makes people wonder: how does a GNN make such decisions, and can we trust its prediction
  with high confidence? When it comes to some critical fields, such as biomedicine,
  where making wrong decisions can have severe consequences, it is crucial to interpret
  the inner working mechanisms of GNNs before applying them. In this paper, we propose
  a model-agnostic model-level explanation method for different GNNs that follow the
  message passing scheme, GNNInterpreter, to explain the high-level decision-making
  process of the GNN model. More specifically, GNNInterpreter\ learns a probabilistic
  generative graph distribution that produces the most discriminative graph pattern
  the GNN tries to detect when making a certain prediction by optimizing a novel objective
  function specifically designed for the model-level explanation for GNNs. Compared
  to existing works, GNNInterpreter\ is more flexible and computationally efficient
  in generating explanation graphs with different types of node and edge features,
  without introducing another blackbox or requiring manually specified domain-specific
  rules. In addition, the experimental studies conducted on four different datasets
  demonstrate that the explanation graphs generated by GNNInterpreter\ match the desired
  graph pattern if the model is ideal; otherwise, potential model pitfalls can be
  revealed by the explanation. The official implementation can be found at this GitHub
  repository.'
